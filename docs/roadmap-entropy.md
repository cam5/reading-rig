# Roadmap entropy — 2026-10-03

How far what shipped has drifted from what the roadmap says. Generated by
`npm run roadmap:entropy` (see [RUNBOOK.md](../RUNBOOK.md#roadmap-entropy))
against `cam5/reading-rig` on 2026-10-03; every number below is that run's
output. The findings and recommendations are a reading of those numbers,
and nothing in the tracker was changed to produce them.

## The roadmap as found

There is no ROADMAP file. The roadmap is the six GitHub milestones:

- **M0 — Scaffold** and **M1 — Reader spine + locators** — closed.
- **M2 — Notebook pane + commonplace book**, **M3 — The Rig** — open, no
  open issues.
- **M4 — Your own library** — open, nothing ever filed against it.
- **M5 — Later** — open, one open issue (#95).

README.md adds the product scope around them: "Personal tool. Single user,
local SQLite, no accounts", and the four invariants. Neither the README nor
any milestone mentions a native client or a paged reading mode;
`ios/README.md` and `lib/paged-columns/README.md` describe those on their
own terms, without tying them to a milestone.

## Findings

1. **The roadmap stopped being used around 3 August.** 84% of merged PRs
   (116 of 138) are unplanned — no milestone, and not closing an issue that
   has one. The weekly ratio climbs from 50–59% in late July to 87% in the
   week of 3 August and 100% in every week since: 75 PRs merged from 10
   August on, none planned. No PR carries a milestone directly; the 22
   planned ones are planned only through the issues they close.
2. **M2 and M3 are finished but still open, and their descriptions are
   out of date.** Both have zero open issues (4 and 6 closed) and both
   still say "Paused pending M1". M1 closed on 2026-07-28.
3. **M4 is empty, though M4-shaped work has shipped.** Nothing has been
   filed against "Your own library", yet #190 ("Let readers upload their
   own EPUBs") merged on 2026-08-17 with no milestone. Whether #190 is M4
   or only part of it is a judgment the script can't make.
4. **The native client is a whole workstream outside the roadmap.** Twelve
   merged PRs touch `ios/` (#198–#200, #202–#210, all on 2026-08-19) and
   none is planned. The API it depends on is in the same position: three
   unplanned PRs touch `openapi/` (#194, #195, #202), and the two open
   issues that follow from it (#192, #196) have no milestone either.
5. **The paged reading mode is one unplanned PR so far.** #212 is the only
   merged PR touching `lib/paged-columns/`. One PR is below the detector's
   threshold for scope creep, so it is listed as a watch item; open PR #214
   continues the same work.
6. **The backlog is unsorted.** 13 of the 14 open issues have no
   milestone, with a median age of 52 days and the oldest (#99) at 61.
   Six were opened in the week of 10 August and two the week after, with
   none closed in either week.

One thing the script does not measure, noted here because it is the same
kind of drift: README.md still says "no accounts", while `app/auth/` and
the magic-link sign-in tracked in #189 are on `main`.

## Recommended actions

Proposals for the tracker's owner. None of these has been done.

1. Close M2 and M3, or replace their descriptions with what is actually
   left in them.
2. Decide what M4 is now: attach #190 to it and file what remains, or
   close it as delivered.
3. Add a milestone for the native client and its API (the `ios/` and
   `openapi/` PRs above, plus #192 and #196), or record that it is
   deliberately outside the roadmap.
4. Decide whether the paged reading mode (#212, open #214) is an
   experiment or a milestone before more PRs land on it.
5. Triage the 13 unmilestoned open issues — most are Rig follow-ups
   (#99, #109, #118–#120, #131, #157, #158) and ingestion fidelity
   (#138, #139), which would fit under M3 and M4 or a new milestone each.
6. Update README.md's scope paragraph to match what has shipped.

The cheapest way to move the score is the second component: sorting 13
issues into milestones is worth about 23 of the 70 points.

## Limits of the measurement

- "Planned" depends on PRs using GitHub's closing keywords. A PR that did
  milestone work without closing its issue counts as unplanned, so 84% is
  an upper bound on work the roadmap didn't anticipate.
- GitHub returns at most 100 files per PR. One merged PR (#147) hits that
  cap; its area is classified from the first 100.
- The score's weights are a judgment call, documented in
  `lib/roadmap-entropy/roadmapEntropy.ts`. Compare it between runs rather
  than reading it as an absolute.
